# Circular Textiles: Product Passport Notes

## 1. Products and materials

A textile product is made of one or more materials. Each material has a material composition that lists its fibres and the share of each fibre as a percentage of the total weight. Common fibres are cotton, polyester, wool and elastane.

A blended material contains at least two different fibres. Blended materials are harder to recycle because the fibres must be separated before they can be reused.

## 2. Substances of concern

Some materials contain chemical substances that are listed on a Restricted Substance List (RSL). A substance of concern is any chemical substance on such a list. For each substance of concern the product passport records its concentration in the material, expressed in milligrams per kilogram.

A product that contains a substance of concern above the limit defined in the RSL is not allowed to be sold in the market where that RSL applies.

## 3. Repair and maintenance

A textile product can be repaired or maintained by a person. A trained person is a person who holds a repair certificate issued by a certification body. An untrained person is a person without such a certificate.

Some repair activities, for example replacing a zipper, may only be carried out by a trained person. Simple maintenance activities such as washing can be carried out by any person.

## 4. End of life

At the end of its life a textile product is collected by a collector and sorted at a sorting facility. Sorting assigns each product to one end-of-life route: reuse, recycling or disposal.

Recycling transforms a product into recycled material. The recycled content of a new product is the share of its weight that comes from recycled material.
